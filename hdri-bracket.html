Which aperture?

    Everything sharp: on a fisheye at f/8, focus at about 1 m. On a 24 mm, focus at 2–3 m. Then switch autofocus off.

    Changing aperture moves both shutter ends with it, so the exposure stays the same.

    ISO

    Lowest native ISO, same for every frame.

            How to shoot an HDRI
            1. Tripod level, camera on a pano head, rotating around the lens's nodal point.
            2. RAW, base ISO (usually 100), white balance fixed, manual focus.
            3. Switch off auto ISO, image stabilisation, long-exposure noise reduction, D-Lighting / highlight tone priority and in-camera lens corrections.
            4. Set the aperture in A / Av mode. f/8 in most cases, never above f/16.
            5. Meter a mid-grey area (grey card, asphalt, grass). Note the shutter time. That's roughly your middle.
            6. Switch to M with that shutter time, same aperture and ISO.
            7. Darkest frame: go faster until the brightest important areas (sky, sun, lamps) are just below clipping on the histogram.
            8. Brightest frame: go slower until the darkest important areas are clearly visible, not black.
            9. Go to Shoot. References at the first angle, then each angle dark to bright.

            Head can't point straight up or down?

            • Tilts 60° or more: 3 shots at +60°, spread evenly around. Same downward.
            • Tilts about 45°: 4 shots at +45°, one every 90°.
            • Tilts 30° or less: add a row at +30°, then take the sky straight up by hand. Sky is easy to patch.

            For an 11 mm APS-C lens in portrait. A longer lens needs more shots.

            Tripod and ground

            • Usually: one normal shot straight down, then paint the tripod out in post. No bracket needed.
            • Your shadow is on the ground: take that shot from another spot.
            • Shiny or bright ground (wet street, water, white floor): it shows in reflections, so bracket it. 3 shots at ±2 EV in burst, 1/60 s or faster, align in post.

            Good to remember

            • Turn around the nodal point, also when tilting. Otherwise close objects shift and the seams show.
            • Shots need to overlap. The app's angle count takes care of it. Don't skip angles.
            • Same bracket at every angle. Otherwise the brightness doesn't match across the seams.
