Which aperture?

    Everything sharp: on a fisheye at f/8, focus at about 1 m. On a 24 mm, focus at 2–3 m. Then switch autofocus off.

            How to shoot an HDRI
            1. Tripod level, camera on a pano head, rotating around the lens's nodal point.
            2. RAW, base ISO (usually 100), white balance fixed, manual focus.
            3. Switch off auto ISO, image stabilisation, long-exposure noise reduction, D-Lighting / highlight tone priority and in-camera lens corrections.
            4. Set the aperture in A / Av mode. f/8 in most cases, never above f/16.
            5. Meter a mid-grey area (grey card, asphalt, grass). Note the shutter time. That's roughly your middle.
            6. Switch to M with that shutter time, same aperture and ISO.
            7. Darkest frame: go faster until the brightest important areas (sky, sun, lamps) are just below clipping on the histogram.
            8. Brightest frame: go slower until the darkest important areas are clearly visible, not black.
            9. Go to Shoot. References at the first angle, then each angle dark to bright.
